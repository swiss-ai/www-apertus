If you've never heard of it, no problem: we had our first public release a year ago, the improved 1.5 update was [deployed in the summer](/articles/2026-07-apertus-1-5). And already, there are several great ways to access Apertus.

**Let's find out how to get started with your hackathon projects!**

## Go local

Apertus is completely free (Apache 2.0 licensed) to download and use on your local or cloud machine. The 8B variant runs well on top laptops, and we even have the [Apertus Mini](/articles/2026-06-apertus-mini) builds for the most modest machines. 

There are instructions for the adventurous --> [Documentation](/pages/documentation/) including free and open source harnesses like Ollama, LM Studio, vLLM and SGlang.

## Think sovereign

![Screenshot from Infomaniak](https://hackmd.io/_uploads/Hye9O1dRWx.png)

If you don't have the hardware, or can't be bothered to download multiple gigabytes of model weights, you can get started with Apertus for free at providers like Infomaniak using their [AI Services page](https://www.infomaniak.com/en/hosting/ai-services/open-source-models). They are a popular choice for many startups right now, with a climate-friendly new data centre in Geneva. We hear you can get a million tokens just for signing up!

![Screenshot of Public AI API](https://hackmd.io/_uploads/S1G5qku0-l.png)

[Public AI](https://publicai.co) is a non-profit which runs hosting for select open models. This is a demo service right now with relatively low rate limits, completely free for testing Apertus. It is quick and easy to get started with a personal API key at https://platform.publicai.co/docs

You can find further providers highlighted at -->  **[Get Started](/pages/get-started/)**

## Connecting academia

The Apertus team is contributing to the development of the Swiss AI Research Platform, where CSCS offers direct access to early versions of Apertus as well as other open-source models to collaborators in the national academic community. [Read more here](https://www.cscs.ch/science/computer-science-hpc/2026/cscs-launches-an-inference-api-service-for-the-swiss-research-community)


![Cluster of computers at RCP](https://www.epfl.ch/research/facilities/rcp/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/cluster-768x576.jpg)

The [ETHZ HPC service](https://docs.hpc.ethz.ch/) has GPU clusters available. For students and staff of the EPFL, we recommmend using the [RCP AI as a Service](https://www.epfl.ch/research/facilities/rcp/ai-inference-as-a-service/) platform. The costs are generally covered by teaching or research grants, and are generally only accessible through the university network or official VPN.

[Renkulab](https://renkulab.io/) from the Swiss Data Science Centre is recommended for members of all Swiss universities. 

## Act global

The [Inference Endpoints](https://digital.swisscom.com/products/swiss-ai-platform/info) of Swisscom's [Swiss AI Platform](https://www.swisscom.ch/en/business/enterprise/offer/platforms-applications/data-driven-business/swiss-ai-platform.html), as well as [Hugging Face Inference Endpoints](https://huggingface.co/docs/inference-endpoints/index), were sponsored at hackathons during the launch of Apertus. They are both an excellent option for developers of demanding applications.

Featherless.ai launched the [Open world](https://featherless.ai/openworld) campaign, hosting both [Apertus models](https://featherless.ai/models?v=c&query=apertus) on their platform. They have generously provided starter keys for our recent hackathons at the EPFL and ETHZ - let us know if you need more access for your hack.

![Screenshot from Featherless AI](https://hackmd.io/_uploads/r1LgtkORbe.png)

As a model compatible with open weight LLM standards, with community builds in GGUF, MLX and other formats, you should have no trouble running Apertus on the AI platforms of various commercial clouds. **Tip:** it is usually easier if they support loading models directly from Hugging Face. 

You can find some recipes in the links in --> **[Get Started](/pages/get-started/)**
